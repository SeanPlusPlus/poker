# Bet Sizing Cheat Sheet

The size of a bet determines the price your opponent gets — and the price you get when facing a bet. Memorize these numbers and you'll never need to do pot odds math at the table for common spots.

---

## The Table

| Bet Size | Equity Needed to Call | Quick Read |
|----------|----------------------|------------|
| 1/4 pot | ~17% | Cheap — call with almost anything reasonable |
| 1/3 pot | ~20% | Still cheap |
| **1/2 pot** | **25%** | **Standard — 1 in 4** |
| 2/3 pot | ~28% | Getting expensive |
| Pot | ~33% | Expensive — 1 in 3 |
| 2x pot | ~40% | Very expensive — need a real hand |

> **The one to memorize first:** half-pot bet = 25% = 1 in 4. This comes up more than any other sizing.

---

## Two Sides of the Same Coin

**When you're the caller:** bigger bet = you need stronger equity to continue. A half-pot bet is a decent price. A pot-sized bet is expensive.

**When you're the bettor:** bigger bet = more pressure on your opponent. If you want them to fold, bet big. If you want them to call (because you have a monster), bet smaller.

---

## Why This Matters

Half-pot bets don't put much pressure on opponents — they only need 25% equity, and a lot of draws and mediocre hands clear that bar. If you want to deny someone a draw, you need to charge more than a half-pot bet.

Conversely, when someone bets small into you, the pot is giving you a great price. Don't fold cheaply — do the math first. You often need less equity than you'd think.

---

## How the Math Works

For any bet:
1. Pot starts at some amount
2. Villain bets X → pot is now (Pot + X)
3. You call X → total pot is (Pot + X + X)
4. Equity needed = **X / (Pot + 2X)**

For a half-pot bet where pot = $200:
- Villain bets $100 → pot is $300
- You call $100 → total is $400
- 100/400 = **25%**

It always works out to 25% for half-pot, regardless of the actual dollar amounts. The ratio is the ratio.
